About this project

An independent guide to Columbus coffee

Columbus on Coffee is an independent, in-depth guide to the coffee shops of Columbus, Ohio — profiling the roasteries, bakery-cafés, and neighborhood counters that are worth seeking out, organized by neighborhood so it's easy to find what's nearby or plan a proper crawl.

Who runs Columbus on Coffee?

Columbus on Coffee is run by Brian M., a coffee enthusiast in Columbus, Ohio.

What's here

Independent and locally rooted stops across Columbus-area neighborhoods, from Dublin and Westerville in the north to German Village, Grove City, and Delaware. Coffee shops, roasters, pop-ups, carts, and places poured around boba or matcha. The list is not a fixed count — people submit places as they use the guide, and accepted ones are added. Each entry includes a neighborhood, address, typical hours, and a short, honest description of what makes the place worth a stop, written from publicly available information and from notes people send in, not from a press release.

What's not here

National chains, mostly. A few multi-location Columbus-born names (Stauf's, Fox in the Snow, Crimson Cup) show up more than once because they've genuinely earned a presence in several neighborhoods — but the drive-thru energy-drink stands and the coffee counters inside big-box stores were left off on purpose.

A note on accuracy

Hours, prices, and details change. This is a snapshot, not a live feed — always worth a quick check before you make the trip, especially for the smaller, owner-run shops that keep unusual or weekend-only hours.

Questions

FAQ

Practical answers. The About page is the story of the guide. This page is how the pieces work.

What is Columbus on Coffee?

An independent guide to cafés, roasters, pop-ups, carts, and boba and matcha stops in the Columbus area. It is organized by neighborhood so you can walk a crawl or find the counter nearest you. It is not affiliated with any shop listed.

Who runs it?

Brian M., a coffee enthusiast in Columbus, Ohio.

Do I need an account?

Browsing is free. Home, the directory, the map, café pages, stories, About, this FAQ, and Flappy Brew never ask you to sign in.

An account is required to submit a shop, send feedback, post a review, upload a photo, suggest a correction, claim a shop, or earn standing and marks. That keeps anonymous notes off the guide.

Where does my account live?

In this copy of the site, the account stays in your browser. A password is stored as a hash, not as plain text. Closing the browser does not delete it, but another device will not see it. A public launch needs a real server before accounts can follow you, and before the site can actually turn bots away.

Do accounts sync to a server yet?

No. Accounts, shop claims, reviews, and photos still live only in this browser. There is no public server login, OAuth, or cloud sync yet. A hosted server is the next step when the site is ready to leave this local copy — until then, treat contributor data as local-only.

What is the difference between Submit and Feedback?

Submit is a structured suggestion for a café or roaster that is missing: name, neighborhood, address, and what to know. It stays pending until an editor reviews it. Once published, it joins the directory and you'll see “Approved” on your Submit page.

Feedback is a note: a wrong hour, a closure, a typo, or a “you should look at this.” It is saved with your account and does not add points. A café you want listed as its own entry still goes through Submit.

Why isn’t my suggestion in the directory?

Suggestions wait. Nothing on this page is added to the public list by the person who typed it. Standing points for a new café (20) or a new roaster (20) apply only after the listing is actually accepted.

How does standing work?

Points add up to a standing: Drop-in, Regular, Neighborhood, then City desk. Reviews, photos, corrections, and likes score right away. A new shop or roaster scores 20 points when an editor publishes it.

  • 20 — a missing café is accepted into the directory
  • 20 — a roaster listing is accepted
  • 8 — review a café
  • 12 — review a roaster
  • 6 — add a photo to a shop
  • 2 — suggest a correction
  • 2 — someone likes your photo, returned if they undo it
What are marks?

Marks are stamps on your signed-in profile. They turn on by themselves.

  • Filed a tip — you suggested a café or roaster
  • Three writeups — three reviews
  • Photo set — two photos
  • Ten reviews
  • Four neighborhoods — contributions in four parts of Columbus
Can I be listed as a contributor?

Yes, if you choose it. After you submit a café or earn a mark, open your account — or the Submit page — and turn on “Feature me at the bottom of the site.” The footer then shows your display name, your standing, and the marks you have unlocked. Your email stays off that line. Turn the option off and the card comes down. On this copy of the site, the row is the people who opted in inside this browser.

What do patrons get?

The order notes on the About page are locked. People who support the guide on Venmo can be given a code. The code unlocks those notes in the browser. It does not sign you in, and it does not let you edit the directory.

How do I claim a shop?

Open the café and choose Claim this shop while signed in. The claim stays pending. Editing hours, the description, and contact info waits until the claim is verified. An editor verifies the claim, usually after a message from the shop's own Instagram or email. Once it's verified, the owner's edits show on the listing right away.

Are boba, matcha, pop-ups, and carts included?

Yes. The directory filters include Boba, Matcha, Pop-up, and Coffee cart. Search also matches those words when they are on a shop’s tags.

How current are the hours?

Hours are a snapshot. Pop-ups and small counters change them often. Check the shop before you go, and send a correction from Feedback if a listing is wrong.

Is the guide affiliated with these cafés?

No. Names are used so you can find the right door. A claim, once verified, is how an owner can correct their own listing. Until then the write-up is the guide’s, not the shop’s.

Is this a business or a nonprofit?

Today it is neither. Columbus on Coffee is a personal project run by Brian M. Support sent on Venmo is a voluntary payment to that person. It is not a charitable donation and it does not buy a share of the guide. If a company, a business, or another organization later runs the guide, the legal page is where that operator will be named.

How can I support it?

The site is free and has no ads. Venmo @brianqzmiller is on the About page if you want to chip in for hosting. It is optional.

How does “Open now” know?

It reads each listing's posted hours and compares them with the current time in Columbus (Eastern time), including late nights that run past midnight. A yellow “Closing soon” badge means less than 45 minutes left. Hours change, and pop-ups move around, so check the shop's Instagram before a long drive. Directory filters also include Early (by 6:30), Evenings (7pm+), and Open Sundays. If a listing is wrong, use “Hours look wrong?” on its page.

How do I plan a coffee crawl?

Tap ♡ Save on the cafés you want, then open the Saved filter in the directory. The guide puts them in a sensible order and opens the route in Google Maps for walking, biking, or driving. “Share this list” sends a friend a link that opens the same cafés, and they can save them all in one tap. Searches share the same way: the address bar keeps your search, so you can copy it. Each neighborhood page also has a “Walk it” route through every café in that neighborhood.

Where do the photos come from?

From the shops themselves wherever possible: their Instagram, Facebook, or website, used with permission and credited with a link to the original post. A few are openly licensed photos credited to the photographer. Owners can add a photo from “Claim this shop.” Visitors can post their own shots in the Photos section of a listing, but only photos they took.

Can I use it like an app?

Yes. On iPhone, open the site in Safari, tap Share, then “Add to Home Screen.” On Android, use Chrome's menu and “Install app” or “Add to Home screen.” It opens full screen, and pages you've visited still load without a connection.

Can I delete my account?

Yes. Open your account, then “Your data.” You can download what you've added or delete the account along with your reviews, photos, corrections, feedback, and pending suggestions.

What is the coffee passport?

Tap “Been here?” on any café you've visited. The home page tracks how many you've tried and which neighborhoods you've finished. In the directory and on the map, “Not tried yet” shows only the places you haven't been. It doesn't need an account, and it stays in this browser.

How do I move my saved list and passport to my phone?

On the home page, tap “Move to another device” under your passport. That copies a link. Open it on your other phone or computer, and your saved cafés and passport stamps are added there. Nothing on the other device is removed. The link lists only café names, so anyone you send it to sees the same list. “Download my lists” saves the same data as a JSON file on your device.

Does Directions open Google Maps or Apple Maps?

On iPhone and iPad it opens Apple Maps. Everywhere else it opens Google Maps. Multi-stop coffee crawls still use Google Maps, because that planner handles a walking route with several stops.

What is Café of the day?

A single café highlighted on the home page for the current Columbus calendar day. It's the same pick for everyone that day, so you and a friend see the same suggestion. It prefers higher-rated shops and changes overnight Eastern time.

Are there keyboard shortcuts?

Press / from almost any page to jump to search (directory or map). Press Esc to close the quick view, the account window, or the phone menu. On a café card, Enter opens the listing, and the small ▢ opens a quick look without leaving the list.

The colors are hard to read. Can I change them?

Yes. At the bottom of any page, pick Light or Dark, and tap “High contrast” for deeper reds and teals behind the light text. If your phone or computer is already set to increase contrast, the guide turns it on by itself. The whole site works with a keyboard, and every map pin is also in the list that comes with the map.

Can I take down one review or photo, or report someone else's?

Your own posts have a Delete link on the café page. The points they earned come back off your total. On anyone else's post, signed-in readers see Report. Reports go to the editors, who can remove the post.
